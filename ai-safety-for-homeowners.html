Use AI for home-project prep without handing it the keys.

AI can organize notes and questions. It cannot authenticate a contractor, inspect a condition, approve a payment, or replace qualified help. This checklist is built for homeowners, older adults, caregivers, and anyone who wants a safer first step without uploading a private message or document.

1 · Pause before you paste

Remove private details first.

Keep passwords, verification codes, Social Security and account numbers, payment details, IDs, medical or customer records, precise access instructions, and anything you do not have permission to share out of an AI tool.

Safer pattern: replace real names, addresses, phone numbers, invoice numbers, and identifying photos with neutral labels such as [HOMEOWNER], [CITY], and [PROJECT].

2 · Verify outside the message

Do not let urgency choose the contact route.

A familiar logo, voice, caller ID, or writing style does not authenticate a sender. Do not use the link, attachment, phone number, or payment instructions inside a suspicious message.

Open a known website or call a number you independently trust. The FTC warns that AI can be used to clone a familiar voice; CISA recommends recognizing, reporting, and deleting phishing attempts.

3 · Keep decisions with people

Use AI as an organizer—not a contractor.

AI may help rewrite observations into questions or place two written quotes into the same comparison fields. It does not diagnose the cause, decide whether an area is safe, verify a contractor, determine permits, review a contract, set a fair price, or approve payment.

If there may be gas, fire, exposed electrical parts, active water near power, unstable materials, or another urgent hazard, stop and contact the appropriate qualified or emergency help.

Known-source check

Ask where each important claim came from.

For any price, rule, deadline, permit, safety, or payment claim, ask for the source and then open the authoritative source yourself. A confident answer can still be incomplete, outdated, or wrong.

Four safer starter prompts

Give AI a narrow job and a boundary.

Use only redacted, non-sensitive information. Read the output as a draft to verify—not a decision.

  1. “Turn these observations into a short list of questions for a qualified contractor. Do not diagnose the cause or recommend a repair.”
  2. “Put these two written quote summaries into the same fields: scope, exclusions, materials, price structure, timing, warranty language, and open questions. Leave missing information blank.”
  3. “Rewrite this project note in plain language. Remove urgency, assumptions, prices, promises, and personal details.”
  4. “Explain this unfamiliar term in plain language, list what you cannot confirm, and tell me which authoritative source or qualified person should verify it.”

Quick answers

Three questions to keep nearby.

What information should I keep out of an AI tool?

Keep passwords, verification codes, account and payment details, identification documents, private records, precise access instructions, and anything you do not have permission to share out of the tool.

Can AI tell whether a contractor message or payment request is real?

No. AI can list warning signs, but it cannot authenticate the sender. Verify through a known website or phone number before clicking, paying, or sharing information.

What is a safe way to use AI for a repair project?

Use redacted notes to organize observations, questions, and comparison fields. Keep diagnosis, safety, contractor, permit, contract, pricing, and payment decisions with qualified people and authoritative sources.
